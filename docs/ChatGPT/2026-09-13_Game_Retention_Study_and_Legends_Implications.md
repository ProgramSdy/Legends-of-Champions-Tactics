# Legends of Champions Tactics — Game Retention Study

**Date:** 13 September 2026  
**Status:** Design discussion / research notes  
**Topic:** Sources of player retention across game genres and implications for Legends

## 1. What We Mean by Game Retention

In this discussion, “game retention” or “黏着性” means the forces that make a player want to continue playing and, importantly, return after leaving the game.

A game can be enjoyable during the first session but still have weak retention if the player feels that they have already experienced most of what it offers.

> **Fun makes the player finish the first battle; retention makes the player think about the next battle after closing the game.**

Different genres create this desire to return in very different ways.

## 2. Fighting Games — Player Skill Growth

Examples discussed: **The King of Fighters** and **Street Fighter**.

Traditional fighting games generally do not rely on persistent numerical character growth. A character does not gain permanent HP, attack power, defence, or stronger versions of the same moves simply because the player has used that character for hundreds of hours.

The character remains fundamentally the same. What changes is the **player**.

Long-term improvement comes from execution and command-input proficiency, combo consistency, timing, spacing, understanding attack ranges and hitboxes, defence and counterplay, matchup knowledge, character knowledge, and decision-making under pressure.

The important observation is:

> **The game piece does not necessarily become stronger; the person controlling it becomes stronger.**

As rosters expand, additional characters also increase matchups and possibilities without requiring RPG-style levelling.

## 3. RPGs — Character and Numerical Growth

Traditional RPGs use a different retention mechanism:

    Start at Level 1
          ↓
    Fight enemies / complete quests
          ↓
    Gain experience
          ↓
    Level up
          ↓
    Increase attributes
          ↓
    Learn skills / obtain equipment
          ↓
    Become stronger
          ↓
    Reach harder content

The player's invested time is converted into persistent character power: HP, Strength/attack, defence, resistance, new abilities, equipment, class progression, and access to new content.

Short-term motivation can be “a little more experience and I will reach the next level.” Long-term motivation can be completing a build, learning an ability, obtaining equipment, or reaching a new area.

Unlike fighting games, much of the progression is stored **inside the character/account**.

## 4. Tactical RPG / SLG — Character Growth Plus Tactical Decisions

Examples discussed: **Final Fantasy Tactics** and **Super Robot Wars**.

These games inherit RPG progression: characters level up, attributes increase, skills become available, units/equipment improve, and harder stages become accessible.

The tactical battlefield adds positioning, team composition, movement, target selection, and ability usage.

Their retention can therefore combine:

> **Persistent character/team growth + tactical optimisation.**

## 5. Early MMORPGs — RPG Progression Becomes a Shared Online World

Examples discussed: **Legend of Mir**, **MU Online**, and later **World of Warcraft**.

Early MMORPGs can partly be viewed as RPG progression moved into a persistent multiplayer environment. Players grind monsters, gain experience, level up, become stronger, access harder areas, and then participate in PvP, guild activity, and other competition.

Online games add retention mechanisms such as other human players, PvP, guilds, group activity, persistent communities, and large-scale activities such as castle wars.

### World of Warcraft

World of Warcraft retained level and character progression but layered it with quests, a coherent world, story/lore, dungeons, raids, cooperative roles, guilds, and persistent social relationships.

This creates another retention force:

> **The player may return not only because “I want to become stronger,” but because “my group is waiting for me.”**

The danger is that progression and scheduled activities can eventually feel like work. Retention mechanisms can become obligations if poorly designed.

## 6. FPS — Player Skill Growth and Competitive Mastery

Example discussed: **Counter-Strike**.

Classic Counter-Strike is much closer to fighting games than RPG progression. Players do not permanently gain more HP, stronger bullets, accuracy bonuses, or statistical advantages simply from playing longer.

Growth occurs in aim, recoil control, movement, reaction, map knowledge, positioning, timing, economy decisions, communication, teamwork, and opponent prediction.

The long-term asset is again largely **knowledge and skill inside the player**. Ranking, team relationships, esports, spectatorship, and social identity add further layers.

## 7. Chess — Strategy Depth as an Extreme Form of Player Growth

Chess demonstrates that a game does not require constant new content, numerical progression, or a growing roster to have extraordinary longevity.

The pieces and rules remain stable, yet players can spend decades improving because the strategic possibility space is enormous.

Retention comes from strategic mastery, pattern recognition, opponent prediction, opening knowledge, tactical calculation, positional understanding, reviewing mistakes, ranking/competition, and enormous state-space depth.

> **Simple rules + enormous strategic depth + continuous player improvement.**

The game does not need to keep giving the player stronger pieces. The player keeps discovering how much more there is to understand.

## 8. Hybrid Retention — Dungeon Fighter Online

Dungeon Fighter Online was discussed as an interesting hybrid. Rather than being purely a traditional fighting game, it combines action/fighting-style execution with online RPG structures.

Its broader retention model can combine real-time action and execution, character progression, equipment, loot, PvE progression, build development, PvP, and social/online systems.

This illustrates:

> **A successful game does not necessarily depend on only one retention mechanism.**

Different mechanisms can reinforce one another, provided they do not undermine the game's core promise. In skill-based competitive games, persistent numerical advantages must be handled carefully because they can damage the fairness that made the competitive experience attractive.

## 9. A Working Model of Retention Sources

From the games discussed, several broad retention models emerge.

### A. Player Mastery
The **player** becomes stronger. Examples: fighting games, Counter-Strike, chess. The persistent asset is knowledge, execution, judgement, and experience.

### B. Character / Account Progression
The **game state** becomes stronger. Examples: traditional RPGs, MMORPGs, many tactical RPGs. The persistent asset is level, attributes, equipment, abilities, and unlocked content.

### C. Strategic Discovery
The player discovers new relationships and solutions inside a stable ruleset. The motivation becomes: **“I have another idea I want to try.”**

### D. Competition
Other humans continuously generate new challenges. Human opponents prevent a game from being completely consumed as finite authored content.

### E. Social Attachment
Relationships create reasons to return: guilds, teams, cooperative groups, and communities.

### F. Content / World Discovery
New areas, enemies, stories, quests, characters, and encounters provide reasons to continue.

### G. Collection and Expansion of Possibilities
New characters, equipment, abilities, or options expand what the player can try. This differs from pure power progression when new content creates **new choices rather than simply higher numbers**.

## 10. Main Insight for Legends of Champions Tactics

After examining these models, the strongest potential retention foundation identified for Legends is:

> **Strategic discovery + player mastery.**

The desired long-term experience is not primarily: “My Warrior has played for 100 hours, therefore his HP and attack are much higher.”

Instead:

> “After 100 hours, I understand my Warrior, my opponents, formations, states, timing, and team relationships far better than I did before.”

A beginner might think: “The Warrior is tough, so I put him in front.”

A more experienced player might think: “In this composition and against this opponent, I can use that Warrior differently because of formation, skill reach, protection, timing, or a state interaction.”

The hero has not necessarily become numerically superior. **The player's mental model has become superior.**

## 11. Progression Can Expand Possibility Rather Than Power

This does not mean Legends must have no unlocks or progression.

A potentially better fit is:

> **Progression unlocks more strategic possibilities instead of simply increasing power.**

Unlocking a new hero should ideally mean: “I now have several new compositions and interactions to explore,” rather than “this new hero has 30% more attack, so my previous hero is obsolete.”

This aligns retention with the broader Legends philosophy that new heroes expand the possibility space rather than replace existing heroes.

## 12. Implication for Pre-Alpha Testing

The early online pre-alpha provides an opportunity to test the core retention hypothesis before layering on artificial retention systems.

At this stage, it may be useful not to rely heavily on daily login rewards, daily quests, battle passes, grind-heavy progression, large numerical upgrade systems, or compulsory repetitive rewards.

Instead, observe whether players voluntarily start another battle, try another composition, change formation, retry after losing, experiment with another strategy, return the next day, return several days later, and use increasingly varied combinations.

The important question is:

> **If we remove external reward pressure, is the battle system itself interesting enough that players want to return?**

If a tester closes the game and later thinks:

> “I just realised another team combination that might work. I want to try it.”

that is strong evidence that Legends' intended retention engine is working.

## 13. Working Retention Hypothesis for Legends

**Primary retention engine:** Strategic discovery + player mastery.

**Supporting retention:** Team composition + formation + skill reach + state interaction + timing + counterplay.

**Longer-term expansion:** New heroes, encounters, strategic options, and modes expand the possibility space.

**Future additional layers:** PvP competition, ranking, community/social systems, collection, and broader progression may strengthen retention later.

The key design caution is that later systems should reinforce rather than replace the core reason to play.

A useful design test for future systems is:

> **Does this feature make the player want to understand and explore the game more, or does it merely require the player to spend more time?**

The first creates durable engagement. The second can create temporary retention but risks turning play into obligation.

## 14. Core Conclusion

Across fighting games, RPGs, tactical RPGs, MMORPGs, FPS games and chess, there is no single universal source of retention.

- Fighting games persist **player execution and knowledge**.
- RPGs persist **character power and progression**.
- Tactical RPGs combine **progression with tactical understanding**.
- MMORPGs add **persistent social relationships and shared worlds**.
- Competitive FPS games persist **player skill, map knowledge and teamwork**.
- Chess persists almost nothing inside the game itself; progression exists largely **inside the player's mind**.
- Hybrid games can combine several retention systems.

For Legends of Champions Tactics, the current hypothesis is that its strongest identity should sit closer to the **mastery / strategic-discovery side** of this spectrum than to a traditional numerical-growth treadmill.

> **“My heroes did not simply become stronger. I became better at understanding what they can do together.”**
