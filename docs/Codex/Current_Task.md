## Requirements

1. **Internal chapter navigation.** Show one chapter at a time, in this order: Quick Start; Battle Formats; Formations & Attack Types; Battle Interface; Turns, Rounds & Victory; Effects & Tactics. Use gold-selected vertical chapter tabs on desktop and an accessible chapter select/dropdown on smaller screens. Provide Previous/Next Chapter controls, natural chapter scrolling, and the existing Back to Manual action. Chapter switching must not call route navigation or change the enclosing Manual dialog lifecycle.
2. **Visual treatment.** Use the established dark-fantasy style: restrained gold, dark panels, serif headings, readable short prose, consistent spacing/width, cards and callouts. Reduce the oversized Instruction title. Keep embedded and standalone layouts readable at desktop and tablet widths without horizontal overflow. Avoid engineering terms in player-facing copy.
3. **Quick Start.** Verify the actual live command sequence from `BattleScreen` and explain acting hero → available skill → required target(s) → available preview → confirmation, with a short illustrated walkthrough and honest exceptions for targetless actions or skills without a preview. Use real UI crops where useful.
4. **Battle Formats.** Compare 1v1, 2v2, and 3v3 with simple team diagrams, size-specific formation availability, and verified completed-round caps (currently 9, 13, and 15 respectively).
5. **Formations & Attack Types.** Diagram both supported 2v2 and all three 3v3 formations, using existing portraits where appropriate and explicit Front/Rear labels. Offer selectable Melee, Ranged Projectile, and Ranged Instant explanations. Verify and show melee rear-target screening and rear-attacker 70% modifier; projectile Front→Front 100%, Front→Rear 80%, Rear→Front 80%, Rear→Rear 60%; and no formation damage adjustment for Ranged Instant. Clearly separate legal targeting, position damage adjustment, and hit chance. State that other skill/target effects still affect final damage. Apply these explanations only to skills with approved attack classifications; do not invent classifications. Render rule-bearing diagrams/numbers in HTML/CSS/SVG, not generated text artwork.
6. **Battle Interface.** Use a current battle screenshot with selectable numbered markers and an explanation panel for team cards, Hero Stats, round/turn order, battlefield targeting, skill cards, previews, confirmation, battle log, speed, Auto Battle, and Resign, where currently implemented. One highlighted region at a time; pointer, touch, and keyboard operation. Capture from the running game if practical. If not, use a clearly labelled schematic of the actual layout, not a fabricated screenshot or broken image. Reuse existing assets and fallbacks.
7. **Turns, Rounds & Victory.** Distinguish one hero turn from a full round, current Agility ordering and verified same-round reordering of unacted heroes. Explain elimination, completed-round limits, finishing the final round, and timeout ordering: elimination, living-hero count, then exact average living-hero HP percentage, then exact-equality draw. Include a worked example where raw remaining HP and average percentage lead to different impressions; verify arithmetic.
8. **Effects & Tactics.** Explain buffs, debuffs, control, periodic damage/healing, casting, and cooldowns using existing status/skill icons and short examples. Link to Hero Gallery for individual skills: the standalone page can use its existing Gallery route; the battle-embedded view must switch within the existing Manual views or direct the player through **Back to Manual → Hero Gallery**, never route away from the live battle. A small internal view callback is acceptable if needed, but external entry/exit and Back behaviour must remain unchanged. Show practical scenario cards as advice, not guaranteed outcomes.
9. **Editable prose and rule authority.** Put editorial chapter prose in structured, schema-validated, human-editable content files, preferably YAML consistent with `web-ui/content/hero-gallery/`. Document where the owner edits it and the rebuild/restart step. Keep mechanical numbers and classifications separately verified against Python/configuration where practical; the client must not implement a second combat rules engine. No new battle API/contract or gameplay change for a content redesign. If a numeric rule cannot be verified, omit or explicitly qualify it and report the gap rather than guessing.
